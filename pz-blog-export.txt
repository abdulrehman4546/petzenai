331	Signs Your Pet Is Sick: 15 Warning Signs Every Owner Must Know	<h2>Why Early Detection Saves Lives</h2>\n<p>Animals instinctively hide signs of illness — a survival behavior inherited from their wild ancestors. By the time a pet shows obvious symptoms, the disease may already be significantly advanced. Knowing the subtle early warning signs can mean the difference between a simple treatment and a serious, expensive emergency.</p>\n<p>This guide covers the most important warning signs for dogs and cats that every owner should know.</p>\n\n<h2>15 Warning Signs You Should Never Ignore</h2>\n\n<h3>1. Changes in Eating or Drinking</h3>\n<p>Any sudden change in appetite — eating more, less, or not at all — warrants attention. Increased thirst combined with increased urination can indicate diabetes, kidney disease, or Cushing's syndrome.</p>\n\n<h3>2. Unexplained Weight Loss</h3>\n<p>Losing more than 10% of body weight without a diet change is a red flag for serious conditions including cancer, kidney disease, hyperthyroidism (cats), or intestinal parasites.</p>\n\n<h3>3. Vomiting or Diarrhea</h3>\n<p>Occasional vomiting can be normal, but persistent vomiting (more than twice in 24 hours), blood in vomit, or diarrhea lasting more than 48 hours requires veterinary attention.</p>\n\n<h3>4. Lethargy</h3>\n<p>If your normally energetic pet suddenly seems tired, reluctant to move, or uninterested in activities they usually love, this is a significant warning sign.</p>\n\n<h3>5. Difficulty Breathing</h3>\n<p>Labored breathing, open-mouth breathing in cats, persistent coughing, or wheezing is always a veterinary emergency.</p>\n\n<h3>6. Changes in Urination</h3>\n<p>Straining to urinate, blood in urine, frequent urination of small amounts, or urinating outside the litter box/trained area can indicate a UTI, bladder stones, or in male cats, a life-threatening urinary blockage.</p>\n\n<h3>7. Eye or Nose Discharge</h3>\n<p>Clear discharge can be normal. Thick, coloured, or excessive discharge suggests infection or respiratory illness.</p>\n\n<h3>8. Scratching, Licking, or Chewing Excessively</h3>\n<p>This often indicates allergies (food or environmental), parasites (fleas, mites), or skin infections. Check for red skin, hair loss, or sores.</p>\n\n<h3>9. Bad Breath</h3>\n<p>While pet breath isn't minty, severely foul breath can indicate dental disease (the most common health problem in pets), kidney disease, or diabetes.</p>\n\n<h3>10. Swollen Abdomen</h3>\n<p>A distended belly combined with unproductive retching in dogs is a sign of bloat (GDV) — a life-threatening emergency requiring immediate surgery.</p>\n\n<h3>11. Limping or Stiffness</h3>\n<p>Occasional stiffness after rest is common in seniors, but persistent limping, sudden lameness, or reluctance to put weight on a leg needs evaluation.</p>\n\n<h3>12. Head Tilting or Loss of Balance</h3>\n<p>These signs can indicate ear infection, vestibular disease, stroke, or neurological problems.</p>\n\n<h3>13. Lumps or Bumps</h3>\n<p>Not all lumps are cancerous, but any new lump should be examined by a vet. Early detection of tumors dramatically improves treatment outcomes.</p>\n\n<h3>14. Pale or Yellow Gums</h3>\n<p>Healthy gums are pink and moist. Pale gums indicate shock or anemia. Yellow gums (jaundice) indicate liver disease. Both are emergencies.</p>\n\n<h3>15.
